- The app runs without Lovable: own Supabase project, Stripe, OpenAI and Firebase are called directly; every key is listed in .env.example. Why: the project is self-hosted.
- Community listings show only groups from the member's own age band; admins also default to their own band and must explicitly opt in to see other bands. Keep the filter in a pure helper with a unit test, and let row-level security remain the real gate. Why: each age band is a separate safe space, and moderation access to other bands should be a deliberate choice.
- Authentication emails go through the Supabase Send Email hook at /api/auth-email (Standard Webhooks signature, sent via Resend) using six templates with shared email-safe theme tokens; keep that route outside app redirects and session checks. Why: account confirmation and recovery must work without an app session.
- Resolve reports through the role-checked atomic respond_report RPC; surface responses only to their reporter through existing report RLS. Why: closing a report and notifying its reporter must be consistent without creating cross-age private chats.
- Open report badges and inboxes share one user-keyed query; private request counts are explicitly participant-scoped. Why: moderation visibility must not create phantom personal notifications.
- Chat voice memos use the shared WAV recorder, private chat-audio storage and message-linked access policies; never transcribe recordings automatically. Why: recipients can listen without exposing recordings or requiring intelligible speech.
- Confirm list deletion inside the app and check the deleted row before updating the selection. Why: native browser confirmations may be unavailable in embedded previews.
- Chat games are rows in chat_games linked from a chat message; only the RPCs start, join, move and stop them, and chat_game_move accepts a move only from the player whose turn it is at the expected move count. Game rules live in pure tested modules under src/lib/chat-games. Why: both players' screens must agree on the game, and nobody can play out of turn.
- Members create groups only through create_group, which places the group in the creator's own age band (at most 3 open groups each); only the creator can change or close it via update_my_group and close_my_group, and admins see new member groups in Nieuws. Why: members can start their own spaces without crossing age bands, and moderators stay aware of them.
- Compliments come from a fixed list and stay anonymous: members only read their own through my_compliments() (no sender), only admins can read the compliments table. Mood entries are private to their owner. Why: kindness features must not become a channel for hurtful or traceable messages, and moods are personal.
- Safety plans, letters to yourself and mood entries are private to their owner; anonymous questions never expose the asker (others only read question_wall()), and their text never goes into a push. Pushes respect each member's quiet hours unless marked urgent (care signals). Meetups are always for one age band. Why: support features must feel safe enough to use, and moderation must not cross age bands.
- Mean words are hidden by the censor() trigger on posts, comments and private messages, using the same list as src/lib/kindness.ts (words with a normal meaning such as 'kanker' stay out); the app asks 'are you sure?' first, and three times in 24 hours flags the member for admins. Goals, gratitude notes and theme entries stay with their owner; chosen theme entries are only shown within the maker's age band. Why: kindness should be nudged, not policed, while care conversations about illness must never be censored.
- Coaches (active rows in coaches, added by admins) may chat with members of any age band and answer anonymous questions without seeing who asked; they show a 🎓 symbol. Members among themselves still only chat within their own band. Why: experienced coaches must be reachable for everyone, while peer contact stays age-separated.
- Moderators (role 'moderator', chosen by the owner via set_moderator in Beheer → Team) only see the Inbox and messages from members; is_staff() gates those tables and RPCs, while organisation/contact messages, the shop, the log and chat overviews stay admin-only. Tab visibility lives in src/lib/staff.ts with a unit test. Why: helpers can moderate safely without access to partner contacts or business data.
- Groups must never feel like spam: no push for ordinary group messages (only replies to your post, @mentions and invites), at most 3 people notified per @mention message (mentionPushTargets), and the slow_down_posts trigger allows 5 group posts per minute per member (admins and coaches excepted). Why: members left earlier groups because they felt spammed.
- CeritaBuddy (the AI chat) only goes through /api/buddy: the server checks login, the read explanation, the on/off switch and the daily limit, runs the safety layer (care, abuse and danger patterns plus a separate Claude safety check, and afterwards the model's own [[veilig]] mark) and answers with Claude via ANTHROPIC_API_KEY and writes every message. Buddy conversations are readable only by their owner, never by admins; only the age bracket goes to the AI, never name or birthdate. The system instruction lives in src/server/buddy/prompt.ts; admins can only add extra instructions in buddy_settings, and the safety rules always come last. Why: young people must be able to talk privately and safely, and safety must not be switchable off.
- Parents and carers have their own account type (profiles.is_parent, set only through become_parent() on an account without a birthdate; a trigger keeps it from being switched by members and keeps their birthdate empty). can_use_community() and my_age_group() exclude them, so they never reach groups, chats, other profiles or CeritaBuddy; the app shows them only the pages in PARENT_PAGES (src/lib/parent.ts, unit-tested). They never see any young person's data, their own child's included. Young members (13–27) who are parents themselves keep their youth account and switch on profiles.also_parent for the parents' corner (/ouderhoek); that changes nothing about their groups. Why: parents get support without making the young people's space feel watched.
- Messages that ask for a phone number, another app, photos, an address, a meeting or secrecy are recognised by src/lib/contact-safety.ts (unit-tested) and the matching contact_risk() in the database: the sender gets a gentle 'are you sure?', members under 18 see a warning under such a message, and repeated signals to under-18s (and new accounts sending many chat requests to under-16s) become safety_alerts for admins; coaches and admins are exempt. Why: adults posing as young people usually start by moving the contact outside the app, and young people must be warned without being policed.
- Parent guides (handleidingen) are pure data in src/lib/guides.ts (unit-tested) and always point to real help (113, 112); a parent's progress, plan and check-in answer (reflection) live in guide_progress, readable only by its owner, and checklist answers are never stored; admins only see totals through parent_portal_stats() (Beheer → Ouderportaal), never who did what. Why: parents should be able to practise difficult conversations privately.
